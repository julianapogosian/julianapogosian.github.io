01 — Selected Work

The Golden Calf

The Golden Calf

Oil on canvas · 80 × 100 cm · 2026

The Golden Calf is the first completed painting in my ongoing series Money Rules the World.

A monumental golden bull dominates the upper part of the composition, towering above a crowd moving toward a distant light. Its surface, built from coin-like marks and layers of gold, transforms the animal into a symbol of wealth elevated almost to the status of a deity.

Below it, the crowd moves through a dark architectural space. Individual figures become part of a collective movement, suggesting how the pursuit of success can overshadow personal awareness and human connection.

The contrast between gold and darkness reflects the tension between the promise of prosperity and the cost of pursuing it. The bull embodies a system of desire, ambition, and power.

When wealth becomes the object of collective devotion, what happens to the people who pursue it?

02 — The Story

Behind the Painting

The first work in the series is The Golden Calf. Dominating the composition is a monumental bull — a symbol of money, power, and wealth embodied in a mythological creature. In my interpretation, however, it is not a living being, but a soulless system that has taken the form of an ancient symbol. Its eyes are empty, yet they emit a mystical glow that draws the viewer’s gaze.

In shaping the bull’s head, I sought to recreate the texture of gold, as though its surface were composed of gold coins. Its horns and nose are made of gold, while golden rivers pour from its nostrils in place of breath, promising extraordinary power to those who manage to reach this monument.

Around the bull stand structures and columns resembling a cage or an enclosed space with no way out. To me, this is a metaphor for the system into which a person is born and within which they follow a predetermined course, guided by an established programme. Over time, they may cease to recognise that this path is not necessarily one they have chosen for themselves.

In the lower part of the painting, I depicted a crowd striving towards the false light of the Golden Calf. All these people seek to reach the summit of wealth. Some seem unable to stop: in pursuit of an illusory goal, they lose their individuality. Some cover their faces with their hands; others are trampled underfoot and go unnoticed. Each figure embodies, in its own way, human fears, aspirations, and inner contradictions.

But what do these people truly sacrifice in pursuit of a dream, luxury, and a beautiful life? Some abandon their childhood dreams, some lose their freedom, while others continue climbing, literally walking over the heads of others. And so the central question emerges: what intangible price will each of them pay, and what will they receive in return?

I deliberately left the figures without eyes. To me, this symbolises blind devotion to an idea and the loss of the ability to see and understand what is happening around us. A person becomes so consumed by the pursuit of a goal that they stop noticing not only others, but themselves as well.

While working on this vast crowd, I felt deeply connected to each figure, as though I were standing before living people, each with their own emotions, anxieties, fears, and dreams. Transferring their images onto the canvas was emotionally difficult for me. Each time I raised my paintbrush to depict another figure, a face without eyes, or someone covering their face with their hands, I felt as though I were coming into contact with other people’s feelings and experiences. The people on the canvas ceased to be mere images — to me, they became alive. This was the most challenging part of the painting.

In my view, The Golden Calf symbolises a system that has endured for centuries.

03 — Artist Statement

My Artistic Vision

My artistic practice grows out of observing the world around me and reflecting on the contradictions of human nature. I am interested in how material values, the pursuit of power, and society’s prescribed ideals of success influence people, change their character and beliefs, and ultimately affect their inner world.

It is impossible to live in this material world without noticing the place money occupies in it. Since my youth, I have observed how money influences human behaviour, habits, and relationships. In pursuing goals dictated by society, a person can seem to follow a road laid out by someone else. Along its winding paths, they may gradually lose touch with themselves and their inner identity.

My reflections on the world around me gradually took artistic form. Observing people, social inequality, and the events shaping contemporary life, I increasingly felt the need to express my response through art. In January 2026, this growing inner tension led me to develop the series Money Rules the World.

Recognising that people can become, in many ways, hostages to the systems in which they live, I wanted to explore what happens to them in the pursuit of wealth, power, and an idealised life. I am interested not only in what people seek to achieve, but also in what they are willing to sacrifice for their goals: freedom, childhood dreams, individuality, or the ability to see and understand others.

I turn to symbolism because I believe it is one of the most expressive ways to convey feelings and ideas that cannot be explained directly. I am drawn to embedding meaning in symbols, details, and images, leaving room for viewers to form their own interpretations. I want people not merely to see a painting, but to feel it and discover something that resonates with their own experience. Symbolism allows me to address complex subjects without restricting the work to a single interpretation.

I had worked with oil paint before, but with this series I chose to move away from creating images digitally and instead engage directly with paint and canvas. It was important for me to express my feelings not through a computer programme, but through traditional painting, where texture, colour, and the physical process itself become part of the artistic statement.

I see myself as an artist who seeks to convey the contradictions of contemporary life through personal experiences, lived emotions, and a sensitive perception of the world. I am interested not only in what happens within society, but also in what happens to the individual within it: how people change, what they lose, what they come to worship, and whether they can preserve their individuality.

I want to continue this investigation and explore these themes more deeply. I cannot yet say with certainty whether Money Rules the World will remain a standalone series or become the beginning of a broader exploration of human nature and society. What I do know is that I feel I have only just begun to speak about something truly important.

04 — Artist Identity

Juliana Pogosian

Contemporary symbolic artist and illustrator.

My work explores the relationship between money, power, ambition, and human identity. Through symbolism and carefully constructed compositions, I examine the values and contradictions of modern society.

My artistic practice includes oil painting and digital illustration. In my paintings, figurative imagery, monumental symbols, and dramatic contrasts of light and shadow transform complex social ideas into visual narratives.

My ongoing oil painting series, Money Rules the World, investigates the influence of wealth on human desire, individual choices, and collective behaviour. Each work approaches this subject through a distinct symbolic scene, inviting viewers to reflect on the systems that shape contemporary life.

Rather than offering simple answers, I use my art to raise questions about ambition, prosperity, responsibility, and the human cost of the pursuit of wealth.

05 — Biography

Biography

Juliana Pogosian is a contemporary artist and illustrator whose practice spans digital art, portrait illustration, and contemporary symbolism. Her work brings together years of professional experience in portraying the human figure with an exploration of social themes through traditional oil painting.

Born in Samarkand, Uzbekistan, Pogosian grew up surrounded by the city’s rich architectural heritage. Its historic monuments, distinctive architecture, and atmosphere became part of her early visual experience. Her interest in drawing emerged in childhood, when she began creating portraits of family members, studying the human figure, and experimenting with humorous illustrations.

In 2008, Pogosian graduated from Samarkand College of Information and Communication Technology and Industry, completing the full course of study in Computer Science and Information Technology. By decision of the State Attestation Commission, she qualified as a specialist in Information and Communication Systems.

Although her initial education was in information technology, creativity had always remained an important part of her life. She developed her artistic skills independently through sustained practice and the study of drawing, composition, and digital painting using educational resources.

In 2014, she began her professional career as an illustrator, specialising in digital portraits and caricatures. She worked at Photolamus from 2014 to 2019 and has continued her professional practice at Caricature24 since 2019, creating commissioned digital portraits and caricatures. This long-term experience has deepened her understanding of human anatomy, individual character, visual expression, and the narrative potential of imagery.

In 2022, Pogosian enrolled at Moscow University for Industry and Finance “Synergy” to fulfil her long-held ambition of pursuing higher education in design. She is currently completing a degree in Design, specialising in Graphic Design and Virtual and Augmented Reality, and expects to graduate in January 2027 while continuing her professional work in digital art.

In 2026, Pogosian turned to traditional oil painting as a means of developing an independent artistic voice. She is currently developing a practice in contemporary symbolism, exploring the relationships between money, power, ambition, and human identity.

Her series Money Rules the World examines how money shapes society, influences perceptions of human worth, and becomes a symbol of power and devotion. Through metaphor, symbolic imagery, and carefully constructed compositions, Pogosian invites viewers to reflect on human nature and the structures of the contemporary world.

She continues to develop her artistic language, bringing together her experience in digital art and her exploration of the expressive possibilities of traditional painting.
